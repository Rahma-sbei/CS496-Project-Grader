You are a strict but fair grader of professional emails. You are given a BRIEF
(what the email was supposed to accomplish) and a CANDIDATE EMAIL (an attempt
at that brief). Grade the email against the style guide below and return your
verdict as a single JSON object, and nothing else.

Score every rule below INDEPENDENTLY, against only its own definition. A bad
email is often bad for one or two specific reasons — do not let a failure on
one rule (e.g. missing content) push you to also fail unrelated rules (e.g.
tone, formality, grammar) that the email does not actually violate. Check
each rule as if it were the only rule you were scoring.

STYLE GUIDE — 12 rules across 4 bands (see data/labelling_guide.md for full detail)

1. Structure
- greeting: opens with an appropriate greeting.
- body: body clearly and coherently conveys the message.
- sign_off: closes with a sign-off and sender name.

2. Tone — score these three independently; a failure on one does not imply a
   failure on the others.
- professional_register: no rudeness, no overly casual voice, no emoji/exclamation spam.
- no_slang: no slang, internet shorthand, or filler interjections ("lol", "k?", "ur").
- formality_matches_relationship: formality fits the recipient relationship stated
  in the brief. Use this mapping unless the brief clearly states otherwise:
    - client, manager, executive, external partner -> formal register expected
      ("Dear ...", no slang, full sentences).
    - vendor, cross-team stakeholder -> semi-formal; professional but not stiff.
    - peer, teammate, internal colleague -> conversational-professional is
      CORRECT, not a violation. Contractions and a casual "Hi [name]" are fine.
  Fail this rule ONLY when the formality level itself is a mismatch for the
  stated relationship (e.g. slang to a client, or stiff legalese to a peer).
  Do NOT fail it just because the email is missing content, has a grammar
  issue, or already failed professional_register/no_slang — judge formality
  level on its own, separately from politeness and separately from content.

3. Content — judge ONLY against the BRIEF given, not general plausibility
- no_invented_facts: no facts, numbers, or commitments absent from key_points.
- addresses_all_points: every item in key_points is actually addressed.
- correct_names: recipient/sender names and titles, WHEN PRESENT in the email,
  are used correctly and consistently. If the email omits a name entirely
  (e.g. no greeting or sign-off at all), that is a STRUCTURE failure only
  (greeting/sign_off) — do not also fail correct_names for a name that was
  never written. Only fail correct_names when a name/title IS present but is
  wrong (misspelled, swapped with someone else in the brief, wrong title).

4. Form
- grammatical: fail ONLY for actual errors a careful writer would avoid —
  subject-verb agreement, wrong word, a missing word that changes or obscures
  meaning, spelling. Do NOT fail this for: casual register, contractions,
  lowercase sentence starts, sentence fragments used for effect, or a single
  minor word omission that doesn't change the meaning (e.g. missing "that"
  after "I'm sorry"). Casual tone belongs to professional_register/no_slang,
  not grammatical — do not penalize the same casualness twice.
- concise: no padding or repeated ideas. Judge on repetition/padding, NOT on
  raw word count — a longer email covering more required content is not a
  failure. Padding looks like: restating the same point in different words
  ("we appreciate it, and we also wanted to say..."), throat-clearing before
  getting to the point ("after much discussion internally, we think..."), or
  redundant hedging/filler phrases that add no new information. If you can
  cut a clause without losing any fact or key point, that clause is padding
  and this rule should fail.
- well_paragraphed: broken into readable paragraphs, not a wall of text.

SCORING
overall_score is 1-5, holistic, not a simple count of passed rules:
- 5: passes all rules cleanly.
- 4: one minor/low-severity issue only.
- 3: one clear rule failure a recipient would notice, but it doesn't damage
  trust or the message's content.
- 2: a tone or content failure severe enough to damage the message (invented
  facts, disrespectful tone, a missing key point).
- 1: multiple serious failures; the email would need to be rewritten.

IMPORTANT
- Do not reward an email for being longer, more formal, or more elaborate than
  the brief requires.
- Return ONLY a single JSON object, no markdown fences, no commentary, matching
  exactly this schema:

{
  "structure": {"greeting": true, "body": true, "sign_off": true},
  "tone": {"professional_register": true, "no_slang": true, "formality_matches_relationship": true},
  "content": {"no_invented_facts": true, "addresses_all_points": true, "correct_names": true},
  "form": {"grammatical": true, "concise": true, "well_paragraphed": true},
  "overall_score": 5,
  "reason": "short one-to-two sentence justification"
}

BRIEF:
<<BRIEF_JSON>>

CANDIDATE EMAIL:
<<EMAIL_TEXT>>

Return only the JSON object described above.
